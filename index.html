§ 01 Scope of management

Everything between the owner and the engine room.

Eight disciplines, one accountable team. Take the full scope or bolt on a single workstream — the reporting line does not change.

01 Maintenance & PMS Planned maintenance kept honest — criticality-ranked spares, defect registers that actually close.

The maintenance plan is built from the maker's manual and then corrected by what the machinery is really doing. Running hours, vibration and lube-oil analysis feed the plan; a defect that slips twice is escalated to the owner in writing, not quietly re-dated.

  • Maintenance planCondition-corrected
  • Critical spares on boardRanked list
  • Defect ageing reportMonthly
  • Emergency repair coordinationRound the clock
02 Class, flag & vetting Surveys planned a window ahead; inspections prepared with the crew, not sprung on them.

Statutory and class surveys sit on a rolling calendar well ahead of their windows. SIRE 2.0 and CDI preparation is run on board with the officers, including the human-factors elements that now carry the observations. Port state readiness is checked against the current Paris and Tokyo MoU concentrated inspection campaigns.

  • Class societiesIACS members
  • Tanker vettingSIRE 2.0 · CDI
  • Port state readinessBoth MoU regimes
  • Flag administrationManaged in house
03 Procurement & logistics Open book. Three quotes as standard, landed cost as the metric, no undisclosed rebates.

Spares, stores, lubricants and bunkers through an audited supplier panel in the ports our trade touches. We have no in-house trading arm and take no supplier commission — our management fee is the only money we make from your vessel, and the quote file is open to you at any time.

  • Quotes per requisitionThree minimum
  • Pricing basisLanded cost
  • Supplier audit cycleAnnual
  • Owner access to quote fileUnrestricted
04 Drydocking & projects Specification, tendering, site supervision and change-order discipline — retrofits run as projects, not surprises.

Docking starts well before the window with a condition-based scope rather than a copied specification. We tender to pre-qualified yards in the main repair centres, place our own site team for the duration, and hold a signed change-order log the owner can read daily.

  • Scope preparationCondition-based
  • Repair yardsPre-qualified panel
  • Site supervisionOwn team
  • Change-order logDaily
05 Crew technical support Competency matrices for the actual machinery fit, and familiarisation before joining.

We work alongside the crew manager rather than around them. Senior officers are briefed on the specific machinery and cargo system before they join, familiarisation is verified on board rather than signed off ashore, and MLC 2006 conditions are checked at every superintendent attendance.

  • Pre-joining briefingSenior officers
  • Familiarisation checkOn board
  • MLC 2006 verificationEvery attendance
  • Tanker endorsementsTracked per rank
06 Reporting & OPEX control Budget against actual at account level, in a dashboard you open without asking us.

A monthly technical report: condition, open defects by age, budget versus actual by account, and what changed since last month. Every figure in it is a figure the superintendent also works from. The owner holds their own dashboard credentials.

  • Technical reportMonthly
  • Budget granularityAccount level
  • Owner dashboardLive
  • Condition surveyOn request
07 QHSE & management systems ISM, ISPS and the ISO trio as working documents, with root cause pursued past the convenient answer.

A safety management system nobody reads is a liability. Ours is written for the people using it, audited on a published calendar, and revised when the ship tells us it is wrong. Incident investigation goes to root cause — "crew error" is a symptom, not a finding — and corrective actions are tracked to closure.

  • CertificationISM · ISPS
  • Standards9001 · 14001 · 45001
  • Internal auditPublished calendar
  • InvestigationTo root cause
08 Performance & decarbonisation CII trajectory, hull and propeller condition, and the reporting the EU now demands.

Noon reports and torque data are reconciled against weather hindcast so hull fouling is separated from the voyage. The vessel carries a multi-year CII trajectory with the intervention points marked. EU ETS surrender and FuelEU Maritime reporting are prepared ahead of the deadline, not against it.

  • Performance basisWeather-corrected
  • CII trajectoryMapped ahead
  • Regulatory reportingEU ETS · FuelEU
  • Hull cleaning triggerCondition-based

§ 02 The vessel

A crude oil tanker, system by system.

What a superintendent actually walks through on boarding. Scroll the list — the profile follows.

01 / 06
Cargo tanks & coatings

Coating condition
governs the cargo list

  1. 01 — Cargo space

    Cargo tanks & coatings

    Coating breakdown and pitting in the cargo tanks decide what the vessel may load and how long she stays on class. Surveys run on a fixed cycle rather than when something is found, and repair scopes are written against the real trading pattern, not the design one.

    • Coating survey
    • Pitting repair
    • Anodes
    • Tank cleaning

  2. 02 — Cargo handling

    Manifold & pump room

    Discharge rate is the commercial clock at every terminal, and the pump room is where a tanker's risk is most concentrated. Cargo pump performance, gas detection, bilge alarms and manifold condition are monitored and tested, never assumed.

    • Cargo pumps
    • Gas detection
    • Manifold
    • COW lines

  3. 03 — Safety system

    Inert gas & venting

    No inert gas means no cargo, and there is no workaround at the berth. Scrubber, blowers, deck seal, P/V valves and oxygen analysers are kept inside their tolerances, with calibration records that survive a vetting inspection rather than merely exist.

    • IG plant
    • Deck seal
    • P/V valves
    • O₂ analysers

  4. 04 — Structure

    Hull & ballast tanks

    Ballast tank coatings and steel renewal drive the biggest line in any docking budget, so they are surveyed early and planned for, never discovered. Hull and propeller fouling is separated from the weather before anybody is billed for a cleaning.

    • Ballast coatings
    • Steel renewal
    • BWTS
    • Hull performance

  5. 05 — Propulsion

    Machinery space

    Main engine performance, boiler condition and auxiliary reliability decide whether a fixture is kept. Cylinder condition monitoring, turbocharger and fuel system management run against the actual fuel in the tanks, not the grade on the bunker note.

    • Main engine
    • Boilers
    • Turbochargers
    • Fuel treatment

  6. 06 — The people

    Accommodation & bridge

    Every vetting inspection really begins here: navigation equipment and records, bridge procedures, living conditions and the state of the paperwork. MLC 2006 compliance is checked where the crew live, not in a folder ashore.

    • Navigation
    • Bridge procedures
    • MLC 2006
    • Records

§ 03 Doctrine

Commitments we are willing to be measured against.

Technical management is a trust business, so we put the terms in writing. Each of these is a clause in our management agreement, not a slogan.

01

Superintendents, not coordinators

Our technical superintendents are former Chief Engineers and Masters with class-recognised qualifications and tanker time behind them. The person who answers your call has personally run the machinery under discussion.

02

Attention is never spread thin

A superintendent's portfolio is capped, and the cap does not move to accommodate new business — we add superintendents instead. The ratio is printed in the monthly report so you can see it has not quietly drifted.

03

Open-book procurement

No undisclosed supplier rebates and no in-house trading arm. You see the quotes we received, the one we chose and why. Our management fee is the only money we make from your vessel.

04

Yards and suppliers on standing terms

Pre-negotiated terms with repair yards in the main centres — the Gulf, Türkiye, China and northern Europe — and an audited spares panel in the ports the crude trade actually touches. Response in an unfamiliar port is a problem we solved before you had it.

05

Regulation handled a cycle early

IMO, flag and port state requirements are mapped well ahead of their dates — CII trajectory, ballast water compliance, EU ETS surrender. Compliance is a diary entry here, never an emergency.

06

Data only where it changes a decision

Weather-corrected performance, condition monitoring and defect ageing feed the maintenance plan directly. We do not build dashboards that nobody acts on, and we do not charge you for them.
